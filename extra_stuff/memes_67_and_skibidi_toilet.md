# Internet memes: "6 7" (sixty seven) and Skibidi Toilet

A quick reference on two big Gen Alpha / "brainrot" memes. Both are mostly used by kids and
teens online and at school, and much of the joke is that they don't really mean anything.

---

## The "6 7" meme (also written 67, 6-7, six seven, sixty seven)

### What it is
"6 7" is said out loud as "six seven", usually with a hand gesture: both palms facing up,
moving up and down in turn, like weighing two things. People shout it whenever the numbers 6
and 7 come up (a page number, a score, a price, someone's height), and often for no reason at
all. It has no fixed meaning. When people do give it one, it's roughly "so-so" or "maybe this,
maybe that", which matches the weighing gesture, but mostly it's an inside joke that's funny
because it's pointless.

### Where it came from
- **The song:** the phrase comes from the rap song "Doot Doot (6 7)" by Philadelphia rapper
  Skrilla, released in late 2024, where "6 7" is a repeated line.
- **Basketball edits:** the song spread through TikTok and short video edits of basketball
  highlights, especially ones featuring NBA player LaMelo Ball, who is 6 foot 7. That linked
  "6 7" to height and basketball.
- **The "67 kid":** in early 2025 a video of a boy at a youth basketball game shouting "six
  seven!" and doing the hand gesture went viral, and he became known as the "67 kid". After
  that the meme exploded among school kids.

### How big it got
By late 2025 "6 7" was everywhere in schools, to the point that teachers complained about
classes erupting whenever 67 came up, and some schools tried to ban saying it. Dictionary.com
picked "67" as its Word of the Year for 2025, describing it as a piece of meaningless slang that
spread through social media and youth culture.

### How people use it
- Someone says a number with 6 and 7 in it: everyone shouts "six seven!" and does the hands.
- As an answer to a question, meaning "eh, kinda" or just to be silly.
- In comments and captions as "67" or "6 7" on anything vaguely related.

## Skibidi Toilet

### What it is
Skibidi Toilet is a YouTube series of short animated videos about singing human heads that
pop out of toilets (the "Skibidi Toilets") fighting a war against humanoid figures with
cameras, speakers and TVs for heads. It became one of the biggest internet phenomena among kids,
and "skibidi" turned into a general slang word.

### Who made it
It was created by Georgian animator Alexey Gerasimov on his YouTube channel **DaFuq!?Boom!**.
The first episode came out in February 2023. The videos are made in Source Filmmaker, the
animation tool built on Valve's Source engine (the same engine as Half-Life 2), which is why
many characters and props look like they're from Valve games.

### The song
The toilets sing a mashup of "Give It to Me" by Timbaland (with Nelly Furtado and Justin
Timberlake) and "Dom Dom Yes Yes" by Bulgarian singer Biser King. The famous lyric is
"skibidi dop dop dop yes yes", which is where the name "Skibidi" comes from.

### The story and characters
The early episodes were seconds long, just a toilet singing at someone. Over time the series
grew into a long running war storyline:
- **Skibidi Toilets:** the invading heads in toilets, from tiny ones to giant ones. The main
  villain is often called **G-Toilet** (the G-Man toilet), based on the G-Man character from
  Half-Life.
- **The Alliance:** the humanoids fighting back.
  - **Cameramen:** people with security cameras for heads.
  - **Speakermen:** people with speakers for heads.
  - **TV Men:** people with TVs for heads, who can stun toilets with what they show on screen.
- **Titans:** giant powered-up versions of the Alliance, like **Titan Cameraman**, **Titan
  Speakerman** and **Titan TV Man**, who became fan favourites.

Episodes got longer, more detailed and more serious over time, with big battles and plot twists,
and the channel built up billions of views.

### Beyond YouTube
- "Skibidi" became Gen Alpha slang. It's used as a filler word, to mean something is cool or
  bad depending on context, or just to sound funny. It's often grouped with other brainrot
  words like "rizz", "gyatt", "sigma", "fanum tax" and "Ohio".
- In 2024 it was announced that Michael Bay and the media company Invisible Universe were
  working with the creator on Skibidi Toilet film and TV projects.
- It spawned tons of fan games, Roblox games, merch and fan animations.

## Why these memes took off

Both memes spread mostly among kids through TikTok, YouTube Shorts and school. They're
repetitive, easy to copy, and confusing to adults, which is a big part of the appeal. They're
often called "brainrot" content, a joking term for internet humour that's deliberately silly,
random or low effort.
